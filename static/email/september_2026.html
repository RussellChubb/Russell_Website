💼 JOB HUNTING

IMAGE PLACEHOLDER

Laptop / Edinburgh / Coffee / Job Hunting

Since getting back from travelling, one of the main projects has been finding a job.

I'm currently looking primarily at Data Analyst / BI / Data Engineering / Power Platform roles, with a particular interest in positions where I can actually build things rather than spend my entire day making PowerPoints about building things.

I've been sending applications, talking to recruiters, tweaking my CV approximately 47 times, and generally trying to figure out what the UK job market actually wants from someone who has spent the last few years working with data.

One thing I've been trying to get better at is treating the job search a little more like an engineering problem:

Apply → Get feedback → Change something → Apply again

Which brings me neatly onto...

🧠 ONE PROBLEM A DAY

IMAGE PLACEHOLDER

LeetCode / DataLemur Screenshot

I've started doing one LeetCode problem and one DataLemur problem every day.

LeetCode is mainly for Python / general programming, while DataLemur is giving me a much-needed excuse to stop pretending I know SQL just because I've been writing SELECT * for several years.

The problems themselves are usually pretty small, but I've found they're good at exposing the gaps in my knowledge.

• Window functions
• CTEs
• Joins
• Aggregations
• Data structures
• Big-O

The goal isn't necessarily to become a competitive programmer. It's more about getting comfortable enough with the fundamentals that technical interviews don't turn into an impromptu episode of Who Wants To Be A Millionaire?

🤖 I AUTOMATED THE JOB HUNT

IMAGE PLACEHOLDER

ApplyPilot / Automation / Job Tracker

Naturally, after doing a few dozen job applications manually, I decided the obvious solution was to automate the job hunt.

I've been experimenting with ApplyPilot, scraping job listings and automating parts of the application process.

There's something quite satisfying about having a machine do the boring parts of looking for work.

There's also something slightly terrifying about realising that I've effectively built a tiny robot whose entire purpose is to help me convince other robots that I am employable.

The important part is that I'm still keeping a human in the loop — the goal is to automate the repetitive bits, not blindly send 400 applications into the void.

🏎️ THE TAKE-HOME TASK

IMAGE PLACEHOLDER

Motorsport Analysis / Chart / Presentation

I also spent a fairly significant chunk of September working on a take-home assessment for Optima / Box Box Analytics.

The task was based around Formula Apex / motorsport content, looking at 2023–2025 championship data alongside YouTube performance.

The question was essentially:

“What should the channel do more of in 2026?”

I ended up looking at things like:

• Which videos actually drive growth
• Race-related content
• Champion / championship content
• Views across different seasons
• What story could be useful for potential sponsors

It was a fun change of pace because it combined a lot of the things I enjoy — data analysis, visualisation, storytelling and making things look reasonably nice.

It was also a good reminder that the interesting part of analytics isn't necessarily finding a number.

It's figuring out what the number means and what someone should do about it.
