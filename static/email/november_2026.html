📚 WHAT AM I READING?

As the colder weather sets in, I've been spending more time reading. Here's what I've been reading this month:

AGAINST THE MACHINE - PAUL KINGSNORTH

This is an interesting book. I hadn't heard of Paul Kingsnorth before, but I was intrigued by the title and cover, so I picked it up.

The book is a series of essays that explore the relationship between humans and the natural world. It also examines how separating humans from nature in modern society alienates us from our own humanity.

Kingsnorth refers to the "machine", a force that is hard to define and describe, but is the matrix of techno-capitalism, industry, and modern society that is driving us away from our natural state. In his words, it's "hollowing us out."

The book challenges the reader to consider their relationship with the natural world, and how they can live a more authentic life in a world that is increasingly dominated by the machine.

Kingsnorth's prose is somewhat technically challenging (not on the level of Nietzsche or anything), but I'm a fool, so I'll probably need to give it a second read to really engage with the text.

I wouldn't say this book is perfect. It starts out quite strong, then seems to regress into pot-shots at certain minority groups, while also calling out the Antichrist in section XX.

Regardless of whether or not you agree about the Antichrist, I think the underlying message is still pretty good.

Bro started out so strong in the first half, then just lost the load

NOWHERE TO HIDE - GLEN GREENWALD

Nowhere to Hide focuses on the story of Edward Snowden and the NSA surveillance programs that he exposed. The first half of the book discusses the events leading up to Snowden's decision to leak classified information, and the second half focuses on the aftermath of the leaks, including the public reaction and the legal and political consequences for Snowden and his allies.

As for my thoughts on the book, I found it very interesting. In particular, I want to note the balls on Edward Snowden: at only 29, he made a decision that would change the course of history, with a clear understanding of the risks involved.

I also want to highlight the NSA's techno-surveillance capabilities. Even back in 2007, those motherfuckers were all over everything: phones, emails, internet traffic—you name it, they were monitoring it. It makes me wonder: "If they were doing this level of surveillance in 2007, with the technology limitations they had then, wtf are they doing now?"

Finally, one of the hardest parts of large-scale data collection is analysing the data. This would have been a problem back in 2007, as they weren't able to leverage LLMs to analyse the data. Now, with the advent of LLMs, they can analyse data in ways that were previously impossible. This is a scary thought.

I want to dive deeper into some of the topics the book touches on in future articles on my website, but for now, just know that I found the book very interesting and a very good read!

Thank you to Bean for lending me his copy of the book!

DESIGNING DATA-INTENSIVE APPLICATIONS - MARTIN KLEPPMANN

If you haven't read my 2026 Data Engineering Roadmap, you may not know that, as part of my personal development, I've identified a number of books (listed in the article) that I want to read to develop my knowledge and skills as a data engineer.

One of those books is Designing Data-Intensive Applications by Martin Kleppmann, which I have been reading this month.

I haven't got too far into the book yet, but it's fundamentally about the design of data systems, and how to build applications that can handle large amounts of data. It covers topics such as data modeling, storage, retrieval, and processing, as well as the trade-offs involved in designing data systems.

If you're not into "computers," this book may not be for you. But if you're one of the folks who joined this mailing list through my blog posts, it'll probably be up your alley!

When I was a young man, I dreamed of optimizing SQL execution plans, designing caching sequences to minimize customer delay when accessing distributed services...
