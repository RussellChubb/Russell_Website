I'm fairly jaded by the world we live in, I'm constantly fed negative information and have a fairly cynical view of the world that can sometimes result in me spiralling into dispair.

I always thought that this somehow placed me above people living on the plane of pure naievity, however, I've recently come to the conclusion that we're actually the same.

This is because both naievity and cynacism result in the same action... no action.

you don't have to believe that you can change the world to recognise that you can change something in it.

Now, you might not be able to dismantle an exploitative economic system.

You might be unable to prevent a government from passing a harmful law.

You might not even be able to convince your friends of something you believe to be true.

But you can help a colleague organise at work. You can support a local journalist. You can vote in a local election that comes down to a handful of votes. You can refuse to participate in a culture that treats people as disposable. You can help build an organisation that gives people more power over their own lives.

None of those things guarantees a better world. They do, however, make a better world more possible.

I have this view that it's impossible to change the world, and that I as an individual have no agency in my ability to shape the world. However, a few things have made me change my stance on this.

## My Views

I have a set of views about the world that I've listed below.

Some of them a true and can be proven by evidence, others cannot.

* Voting provides the illusion of choice, however, nothing will fundamentally change, until our underlying system fundamentally changes. This will likely never happen. However, not voting will make for a passive, skeptical population.

* There are systematic efforts to keep you just broke enough to work, just tired enough to not unionize, and just entertained enough to keep you from rioting.

* We used to live in accordance with the world, i.e. we were it's guardian's. The modern world has flipped that relationship, and we now believe that the world is a set of resources to be harvested.

* There have been efforts made to divide us as much as possible into race, gender, sexuality, voting preference. Anything and everything that is possible to segment by, we have been segmented by. This is done for the purpose of keeping us working together to see that the only real difference is those with, and those without.

* The power of the US state is unlimited and ultimate. Trying to fight with force, or intel is impossible, you're fighting an unrelenting machine. There is no fight.

* Social psychologists have a deep and pervaisive understanding of the human mind and what makes it tick. There are tactics deployed that work on you regardless of whether or not you know about these tactics i.e. propoganda. These tactics are exploited in media, in technology (*think social media applications*).

* The media is currently bought and owned by the ruling class of the world. Only small independent journalism has a sembalence of being trusted.

* You can no longer trust anything being posted on the internet. Bot Traffic now makes up the majority of traffic on the internet, and now that bots are LLM + Generative AI enabled, anything can be faked, you can be made to say anything on the internet and there's nothign you can do about it. More-over, you can no longer trust any text on the internet. Bot storms are sweeping the internet and are able to subtly influence your perception of the world.

* You are under constant surveillance, whether you're interesting or not. Every single app tracks you. You have a listening device in your pocket right now. Every transaction, every store you go to. Companies like Palantair are tracking you from the moment you're born to the moment you die. All to build a profile on you to better advertise, or blackmail you.

* There are terrors that governments have access that are beyond your nightmares. You only know about things in the public record, there are things out there will haunt you.

With all of this being said, naiviete is just as bad as skepticism in the way that both enable a lack of effort towards fighting the fight, regardless of whether or not you'll win. I always thing about this guy who when asked "*what's the point of recyling / consuming less when companies will still polute etc etc*" he basically responded by using virtue ethics to justify that whether or not companies / other people will polute the environment, the morally right thing to do is try for the sake of trying.

It's this line of logic that I think we need to embody in the world we live in. You need to breathe in the reality that we're unlikely to "win", but atleast we went out with a fight.

## What can we do to fight back?

Well, this is something that I don't really fully understand. How do you actually fight back?

There are three big categories:

* actions that reduce your personal exposure to harmful systems
* actions that redirect resources
* actions that build collective power.

* Reduce spending. Spending on frivalous silly things and perpetrating a culture of consumption will continue to maintain the status quo.

* Reduce IoT devices from your home.

* Use cash to pay for things.

* Support small, local business's.

* Don't watch the news.

* Minimize, or eliminate social media usage.

* Show up to local debates, and more generally get involved in local politics.

* Research policy for various parties, and don't be afraid to vote for a minority party.

* Arrive on time and leave at the end of the day.

* Join trade unions. You spend so much of your life working, you need to be united as workers towards higher pay and better working conditions.

* Get to know your neighbours. A system that leaves people isolated can be challenged by people who know and trust each other.

* Practise collective, not just individual, environmental responsibility.

* Learn how decisions are actually made. Pick one thing and learn about how the levers of parliament actually work.

* Defend the conidtions of fredom. Democracy, freedom of association, freedom of expression, privacy, independent courts, and access to reliable information aren't solutions to every problem. But they make it possible to challenge power without having to defeat it through force.

Defending these institutions matters even when they are imperfect, and even when the political side you favour isn't the one benefiting from them.

This is where I would challenge the idea that the only meaningful fight is one against the entire system. Sometimes the most important work is preserving a mechanism through which people can challenge the system in the future.
